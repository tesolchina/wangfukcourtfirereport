## What it argues
1. **The Government's self-description:** the relevant bureaux and departments — "namely the Police, FSD, LD, BD, ICU of the HD, and HAD" — are represented by the DoJ and counsel, and took "a proactive approach to cooperate with and support the work of the Committee".
2. **Witness characterisation:** "**27 factual witnesses being current or former officers and representatives from different Bureaux and Departments of the Government, gave oral evidence**" (in addition to written statements).
3. **Its own reform narrative:** ending remarks refer to improvement measures "already implemented or soon to be implemented" and further review "in the major policy areas covering at least the MBIS, MWCS and establishment of concrete joint efforts in handling enquiries and complaints"; the Government "pledges to respond positively, substantively and expeditiously" to the Committee's recommendations.

## Counter-evidence note (important for our thesis)
The Government calls its witnesses officers "from different **Bureaux** and Departments". The
Committee's own timetable shows what that means in practice: **no bureau secretary, permanent
secretary or under-secretary** appears; the 27 are department-level officers (FSD, BD/ICU, LD,
HAD, Police). So "bureaux and departments" is the Government's collective label for its
administrative side, not evidence that policy-makers testified.

Part III(F) ("Systemic issues") is the section where the Government most directly engages policy;
it needs a close read before the letter asserts anything about the Government's policy defence.
