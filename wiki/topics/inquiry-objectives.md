# What each hearing block was for — objectives (inferred) and facts surfaced

Purpose of this page: not "what the documents say" but **why each session existed** and **what it
established**. Objectives are *inferred* from (a) the Committee's Terms of Reference, (b) the
witness roles in its published timetable (as at 24 Jun 2026), (c) the line of questioning visible
in the transcripts (formulation-vocabulary and topic-term counts), and (d) the closing
submissions, which reveal what counsel ultimately sought to prove.

## Framework — the four objectives in the ToR
1. **Establish the facts of the fire** (causes, spread, casualties).
2. **Test the fire-safety systems and their supervision** (installations, operation, responsibility).
3. **Test the maintenance/renovation regime** (standards, materials, verification, testing).
4. **Examine systemic problems and bid-rigging**, judge the **adequacy of regulation and penalties**,
   and **recommend** (ToR 3 and 4).

The hearings were organised by *actor*, not by objective — which is why each block below maps
onto more than one ToR.

## Block 1 · Residents and residents' representatives (T3–T5, T13; 24–30 Mar, 16 Apr)
- **Witnesses:** 24 residents; cleaners and site workers.
- **Objective (inferred):** establish the lived facts — what residents saw and heard (no alarm,
  smoke in stairwells), what they had complained about (smoking on scaffolds, the renovation
  contract), and what they were told.
- **What it established:** complaints existed and were repeated; the alarm failure was
  experienced, not merely recorded; residents' opposition to the renovation contract predated the fire.
- **Policy content: almost none** (T4: 1 formulation term in 138k characters). Residents testify to
  events, not to rules.

## Block 2 · Contractors, consultants and the works chain (T6–T9; 31 Mar–8 Apr)
- **Witnesses:** management-company staff, fire-service contractor staff, subcontractors, the
  main contractor's director.
- **Objective:** reconstruct the works — who was engaged, by whom, on what terms; how shutdown
  notices were filed; how materials were specified.
- **What it established:** shutdown notices filed without site visits (85); an oral subcontract
  with no written contract; a false fire-retardancy certificate used for replacement nets;
  responsibility diffused through layers of subcontracting.
- **Policy content: moderate** (standards/guidelines discussed with the people who complied — or did not).

## Block 3 · Fire Services Department (T10–T12, T17–T19; 10–24 Apr)
- **Witnesses:** Assistant Commissioner (licensing), district officers, Deputy Director (Operations),
  Director of Fire Services.
- **Objective:** test the FSD's inspection and enforcement regime and its operational response.
- **What it established:** the "current policy … no surprise checks"; the 1982 code of practice on
  inspection and testing; how alarm shutdowns were notified and not verified; the 999 call-handling
  failure (T12: 121 formulation terms, mostly *guideline*).
- **Policy content: high** — but always with the department that applied the policy.

## Block 4 · Buildings Department, ICU, Home Affairs, URA (T20–T24; 27 Apr–8 May)
- **Witnesses:** BD Assistant Director (Mandatory Building Inspection), BD surveyor seconded to the
  ICU, ICU senior building maintenance surveyors, ICU Director, HAD district officer and Deputy
  Director, URA rehabilitation staff.
- **Objective:** test the building-control chain — who set the standards, who checked compliance,
  who followed up complaints.
- **What it established:** the ICU "policy-wise … follow[s] the BD's practice — to put it bluntly,
  blindly"; certificates accepted without a desktop check; complaints passed between departments.
- **Policy content: highest of any block** (T20: 130 formulation terms; 條例 73 in Chinese). Yet the
  policy *authors* — the bureaux — were not in the room.

## Block 5 · Experts, task force and agencies (T25–T27; 22–25 Jun)
- **Witnesses:** Usmani and Jiang (fire engineering), Government Laboratory, the inter-departmental
  task force, a government-appointed expert.
- **Objective:** establish cause and spread scientifically; test the adequacy of standards against
  fire-engineering evidence.
- **What it established:** the probable cause (cigarette/cartons, smouldering); the physics of
  vertical spread via re-entrants and materials; standards critiqued by experts, not by rule-setters.
- **Policy content: high in the standards sense** (T25: 59 mentions of *standard*) — but as
  engineering critique, not as an examination of the decisions that set the standards.

## Block 6 · Closing submissions (T28–T30; 15–17 Jul) and the written closings
- **Objective:** allow each party to argue what the evidence shows.
- **What it established:** the Committee's counsel's synthesis (Ch7 §1187 assigning responsibility
  to ICU/BD, FSD, LD), and the reform agenda (Ch8, Ch10). The Government's counsel argued the
  policy side in 408 pages.
