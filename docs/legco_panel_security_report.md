Dear Sir/Madam

**Re: The Wang Fuk Court fire — following up the Independent Committee's findings with the Security Bureau**

The fire at Wang Fuk Court, Tai Po, on 26 November 2025 killed 168 people, including firefighter Ho Wai-ho, and left thousands homeless — the deadliest fire in Hong Kong in more than 70 years. Wang Fuk Court is a Home Ownership Scheme estate of eight 31-storey blocks completed in 1983 and home to some 4,600 residents; a HK$330 million renovation project by Prestige Construction and Engineering under the consultancy of Will Power Architects began in July 2024. The Independent Committee in relation to the fire at Wang Fuk Court, appointed by the Chief Executive on 12 December 2025 under the chairmanship of Mr Justice David Lok, held six rounds of public hearings between March and July 2026 and, following an extension granted on 18 August 2026, is expected to submit its report to the Chief Executive by the end of October 2026. Its published record already comprises 243 documents — 6,377 pages and 1.9 million words of witness statements, hearing transcripts and expert reports, together with some 1,800 photographs and diagrams.

That record shows how nearly every safeguard failed on the day. As the Committee's lead counsel, Mr Victor Dawes, put it to the hearings, "on the day of the fire, nearly all fire safety systems meant to protect lives failed because of human factors". The fire alarms in seven of the eight residential blocks had been deactivated and the fire-service water tanks emptied; a fire-service contractor filed 85 shutdown-renewal notices without once visiting the estate; flammable foam boards covered the windows and non-fire-retardant netting had replaced typhoon-damaged nets; a registered inspector was described in evidence as acting as a mere rubber stamp; and a renovation subcontract was agreed orally, with no written contract. Committee counsel identified a lit cigarette on the flat roof of the light well outside flats 104–105, Wang Cheong House, igniting combustible cartons, as the probable cause of the fire. On 10 June 2026, two companies and seven individuals were charged with a total of 25 offences including manslaughter; the police have arrested 35 people and the Independent Commission Against Corruption 23.

While the Committee's recommendations are awaited, several matters already fall within this Panel's oversight of the security portfolio. We urge the Panel on Security to —

(a) invite the Security Bureau and the Fire Services Department to brief the Panel on the Committee's findings and recommendations as soon as the report is published, and to table the report to the Panel;

(b) examine the notification regime for the shutdown of fire service installations, under which a contractor could file 85 renewal notices without any site visit, and ask the Fire Services Department how shutdowns and restorations will in future be verified by physical inspection before residents have to depend on those systems;

(c) ask what mechanisms exist to suspend or debar fire-service and renovation contractors implicated in the inquiry pending the outcome of prosecutions, noting that two fire-service contractors linked to the tragedy were reported in August 2026 to be still in business and taking new contracts;

(d) request a public timetable for implementing the Committee's recommendations, including any legislative amendments found necessary under the Committee's third term of reference on whether regulation and penalties under relevant existing laws are adequate, with progress reported back to the Panel; and

(e) encourage the Administration to present the inquiry record and its response organised by the Committee's Terms of Reference, so that members and the public can trace each recommendation to the underlying evidence — the accessibility concern set out in our letter to the editor at the Appendix.

As the Buildings Department falls under the Development Bureau, we also suggest that the Panel consider liaising with the Panel on Development on the building-works aspects of the Committee's findings.

To ensure that the 168 lives lost at Wang Fuk Court were not lost in vain, we urge the Panel on Security to follow up on this matter with the Security Bureau.

Yours sincerely

Simon Wang

**Appendix**

*Fire inquiry documents should be more accessible*
(South China Morning Post, Letters, 19 August 2026)
<https://www.scmp.com/opinion/letters/article/3364395/hong-kong-mustnt-use-wrong-index-gauge-outdoor-heat-stress#:~:text=Fire%20inquiry%20documents%20should%20be%20more%20accessible>

Sir,

The Independent Committee on the Wang Fuk Court fire has published a large body of material - witness statements, hearing transcripts, expert reports and photographs. The site orders them by the sequence of the committee's work, not by the tasks outlined in its Terms of Reference. Journalists and the public seeking evidence on sprinkler failure or tender collusion must wade through page after page.

To test whether it could be made navigable, we used AI tools: a web crawler collected every document from the site, and large language models read, summarised and tagged each against the seven tasks in the Terms of Reference - including OCR for the 27 documents that arrived as scans with no readable text.

The results are striking: 243 documents, 6,377 pages, 1.9 million words, roughly 1,800 photographs and diagrams. Yet the material is unevenly distributed: supervision responsibilities appear in 195 documents, adequacy of laws in 77, and bid-rigging and collusion - what residents most want explained - in 99, against eight and eleven in a summary-level first pass. Nor is that all: reading the full text, we found evidence bearing on tender irregularities in documents that never use those words - a fire-service contractor that filed shutdown-renewal notices without ever visiting the estate, a renovation subcontract agreed orally with no written contract, and a contract attachment whose material-data folder was empty. A keyword search, or the committee's own menu, would never surface them. Every document is public, but the questions people ask are the ones the archive makes hardest to answer.

To help readers answer them, we have built a free bilingual site (https://wangfukcourtfirereport.simonsays.hk/) organised around the seven tasks. Each document has a page with a summary, key points, a table of contents and a link to the original PDF; an interactive map shows how the documents relate to each task, and full-text search spans all 243 documents. We urge the independent committee to adopt such tools in presenting its findings in a more accessible manner.

Wenyun Fu and Simon Wang, Kowloon Tong

## Submission notes (not part of the letter)

1. **How to submit** — email the letter (as PDF, signed) to `panel_s@legco.gov.hk`, following the 2021 minibus-letter precedent: request (i) circulation to Panel members and (ii) publication on the LegCo website, then ask for the paper number and link once issued. Cc the Panel's clerk. If no acknowledgment follows, follow up (the 2021 letter needed a chaser after two months).
2. **Paper number** — `CB(2)____/26-27(__)` is a placeholder; the Secretariat assigns it when the letter is circulated as a panel paper. Panel on Security papers use the CB(2) series (e.g. `se20260924cb2-1183-1-e.pdf`).
3. **Co-signatories** — add names from the editors' contact list prepared by Winnie (shared Google Doc: <https://docs.google.com/document/d/1ds3rZy3Xgb9dxvK6-FcPoyNVjiXGtwDuYNpLZoG489g/edit?usp=sharing>).
4. **Timing** — the Committee's report is due by end of October 2026. Sending the letter in the week the report is published (or immediately after) maximises the chance the Panel puts the briefing on its next agenda.
5. **Chinese version** — the letter is English-only for now ("English version only", as with the sample). A Chinese version can be prepared if co-signatories prefer bilingual submission.
6. **Tone guardrails** — the Committee's own note states that legal liabilities are matters for the law-enforcement agencies and outside its scope; the letter therefore reports the evidence and the charges as published facts and directs its asks at the Panel's oversight role, not at the courts.
