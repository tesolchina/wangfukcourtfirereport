# Meta-read notes — Wang Fuk Court inquiry record

Systematic reading of the Committee's published record, one document group at a time.
Purpose: establish what data and evidence the final report will draw upon, and test the
hypothesis that the inquiry leans on **policy implementation** actors rather than
**policy formulation** decision-makers.

Sources: PDFs downloaded from the committee site (`/tmp/closings_pdf/`), text extracted to
`/tmp/closings_txt/`; manifest `data/manifest.json`; witness timetable (as at 24 Jun 2026).

**Chapter 7 — the decisive finding.** The chapter analyses government responsibility
"from the perspective of paragraph 1 of the Terms of Reference". It mentions **"Bureau"
zero times**. Its conclusion (para 1187):

> "The Committee is accordingly invited to find that, among the Government bodies before
> it, the responsibility for the fire-safety failures lay with the **ICU/BD, the FSD and
> (in respect of occupational fire risk) the LD**."

So: responsibility is allocated to **three departments plus the ICU** — exactly the four
bodies reported in the press — and to **no bureau**. Within the same chapter, "policy" is
discussed 26 times and "standards" 31 times: the chapter *argues* about standards and
policy adequacy, but *allocates* responsibility only at department level.

**Chapter 8 — policy-level recommendations.** Contains distinct sections
"Recommendations for Immediate Implementation" and **"Recommendations for Long-Term and
Systemic Policy Reforms"**. So the recommendations reach policy design.

**Chapters 9–10 — bid-rigging.** Ch9 examines the systemic landscape (with the express
caveat that the Committee is "not required by the Terms of Reference to make findings on
the existence of such issues in the case of Wang Fuk Court"); Ch10 recommends market
integrity/gatekeeping, enhanced legal deterrence and professional discipline, proxy and
voting reforms.

**The asymmetry this establishes:** *responsibility is assigned to departments, while the
recommendations propose policy reform.* Accountability and remedy sit at different levels.

### 1.4 Working conclusion from Group 1
The inquiry's **evidence** is department-, contractor-, resident- and expert-level; its
**responsibility findings** are department-level (ICU/BD, FSD, LD); its **recommendations**
are policy-level. No bureau-level official gave evidence and no bureau is named in the
responsibility chapter. The defensible claim is therefore:

> *The inquiry heard from the people who applied the rules, not the people who wrote them —
> and it recommends rewriting the rules while assigning responsibility only to the
> departments that applied them.*
