Each number is how many of the 243 documents contain substantive material on that task, judged by reading the full text in passages (semantic judgment, not keyword matching; a document may count under several tasks). 3. Yes — here are the documents, with the exact pages and excerpts.

None of these uses the words "collusion" or "bid-rigging". The evidence lies in the conduct each document describes — conduct that, read in the context of the Committee's Terms of Reference on "connected interests, collusion and bid-rigging", reveals the systemic patterns the Committee was asked to investigate:

  • A contractor filing duplicate shutdown notices — the oversight mechanism reduced to paperwork. China Development Engineering, a registered fire-service contractor, submitted the same shutdown notice twice on the same day — identical content, different extension dates — for each of the eight blocks. The notice did not mention that the fire-alarm system would also be shut down. This is not a contractor assessing risk on site; it is a contractor mechanically filing paperwork to extend a shutdown, month after month, without flagging that the alarm system was disabled. The fire-service shutdown regime — designed to let the Fire Services Department monitor safety — was being used as a rubber stamp. 1st Witness Statement of Mr CHUNG Kit Man, p.6 — link. Excerpt (p.6): "中華發展工程有限公司同日另提交同一份通知，內容相同，但指維修工程將延長至 2025年11月27日" (China Development Engineering submitted the same notice on the same day, identical content, but stating the repair works would be extended to 27 November 2025).
  • A registered contractor who never visited the site — hired to sign, not to assess. China Development was engaged by the main contractor (宏業) to file shutdown notices, but never visited Wang Fuk Court, never assessed the need or consequences of shutting down the fire installations, and admitted in evidence to acting as a "rubber stamp". The main contractor, which was not itself a registered fire-service contractor, hired China Development solely to submit the paperwork — not to provide independent professional judgment. This is the structural pattern the Committee's ToR2 targets: parties that should be checking each other instead covering for each other. Synopsis of Closing Address (Government), p.16 — link. Excerpt (p.16): "中華發展工程有限公司（「中華發展」，一間註冊消防裝置承辦商）：從未到訪現場、從未獨立評估關閉消防裝置的需要及後果、僅作為「橡皮圖章」提交關閉通知書" (China Development Engineering, a registered fire-service installation contractor: never visited the site, never independently assessed the need or consequences of shutting down fire installations, acted merely as a "rubber stamp" in submitting shutdown notices).
  • An oral subcontract with no written contract — procurement without a paper trail. On Cheong Engineering's facade-sealing subcontract was agreed orally with an intermediary (新南方建築), not directly with the main contractor (宏業), and no contract was signed. An oral subcontract with no written terms makes it impossible to audit who was hired, at what price, for what scope — the kind of opacity that enables inflated pricing, substitution of materials, and directing work to compliant firms outside any competitive process. Reply from On Cheong Engineering, p.7 — link. Excerpt (p.7): "新南方建築有限公司口頭與王志鑫先生達成工程的分判工程，期間並沒有簽訂任何合約" (Sun Nan Fang Construction Company orally agreed on the subcontract with Mr Wang Zhi-xin; no contract was signed).
  • All work directed by the main contractor — subcontractors with no independence. Tin Hung Engineering states that "all work procedures were arranged by the main contractor (大判) and we followed instructions." When the main contractor controls all work allocation, subcontractors cannot bid competitively, challenge instructions, or refuse non-compliant work — they execute what they are told. This concentration of control is a structural feature that enables collusion: the main contractor can direct work to compliant firms and exclude others, and subcontractors have no incentive to raise safety concerns. Reply from Tin Hung Engineering, p.6 — link. Excerpt (p.6): "所有工作程序由大判安排，我們依照指示進行" (All work procedures were arranged by the main contractor; we followed instructions).
  • An empty material-data folder — the contract record was incomplete. The material-data folder ("DWG4") on the disc accompanying the signed contract was found to be empty. Missing contract documentation prevents any audit of what materials were specified, quoted, or delivered — the kind of gap that enables substitution of non-compliant materials (as happened with the non-fire-retardant netting and foam boards) and makes it impossible to verify whether the procurement was genuine. 2nd Supplemental Witness Statement of Mr LEE Kwok Hung, p.4 — link. Excerpt (p.4): the material-data folder "DWG4" was found empty on the disc accompanying the signed contract.

Each of these describes a facet of the same pattern: parties that should provide independent oversight instead covering for each other; procurement conducted without written contracts or auditable records; and a main contractor that controls all work allocation while hiring registered professionals solely to sign paperwork. Read together, they show why the Committee's Terms of Reference asked about "connected interests, collusion and bid-rigging" — and why a keyword search for those terms would not find them.
