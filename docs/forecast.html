Update — 21 October 2026 (HKT)

The South China Morning Post reported on 20 October (Natalie Wong and Jeffie Lam, citing sources) that the Committee will submit its final report to the Chief Executive on Thursday, 22 October, issuing a statement that day with no press conference — mirroring the 2013 Lamma Island ferry inquiry. A redacted version is scheduled for public release next week at the earliest.

  • The panel is expected to largely endorse lead counsel Victor Dawes SC's 627-page closing submission: systemic failures and a blatant disregard for safety, an “unshirkable responsibility” on government — naming the Fire Services Department, Buildings Department, Labour Department and the Housing Bureau's Independent Checking Unit — and the contractors' “strongest condemnation” for non-flame-retardant materials.
  • Bid-rigging will be addressed but is not expected to be a “major focus.”
  • DoJ will redact to avoid prejudicing the 25 criminal charges against seven individuals and two companies; redactions are expected to be “relatively insignificant”, and civil servants' names will not be redacted (already disclosed at the public hearings).
  • Disciplinary action is left to the administration; insiders say the groundwork was laid earlier this month, and the timing was “carefully calibrated” around the APEC finance ministers' meeting and the 22 November Election Committee election.

What this means for the forecast: predictions 2, 3, 4, 7 and 11 sit close to Dawes's closing submission and should be read as likely confirmed; prediction 6 (tender irregularity) should be downgraded from “described in detail” to “addressed, not central”; prediction 8 (debarment/discipline) is now a question of speed, not existence — insiders expect action quickly. Predictions 1 (cause), 5 (registered inspector), 9 (emergency response) and 10 (residents) are untouched by the report's form, and will be tested against the text itself.

What would change the forecast

  • New documents before publication — our audit of the Committee site runs on demand; a fresh batch would show as new pages > 0 (audit log).
  • The 15 October hearing material — the 999 recording; if call-centre protocol is taken up formally, expect a finding alongside prediction 9. Prior art: search: 999 → 15 Apr transcript, 105 hits.
  • Redacted appendices — would weaken the evidential trail behind prediction 6, which is exactly the accessibility argument in our submission to the Panel.
