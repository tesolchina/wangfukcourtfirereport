What the record's own words show

Word and phrase frequency analysis of the Independent Committee's published record — 243 documents · 6,377 pages · ~1.9 million words — contrasted with the language of news coverage (69 article bodies, 20–25 October 2026). Method, tables, six arguments, the department map, and the full article list.

Where every number comes from
COMMITTEE SITE — the Independent Committee's own archive: 243 documents · 6,377 pages · ~1.9m words (counts from our full-text index; phrase counts from the 111-document text mirror).   NEWS — published news articles, 69 with usable bodies, 20–25 Oct 2026 (counts from the article texts; full list and selection rules in §8).
Sections 2, 3, 5 and 6 are SITE unless a line says otherwise; §4 sets the two side by side; every NEWS figure can be traced to a row in §8.

1 · Method — how each side was counted

  • COMMITTEE SITE Corpus. Our text mirror of the official archive: all 243 PDFs converted to text (347 scanned pages OCR'd), held in an inverted index (term → per-document frequencies).
  • Filters. Transcript boilerplate (simultaneous interpretation · ambiguity · discrepancy · versions prevail · evidential hearing · witness statement), English stop-words, and personal names (counsel, witnesses, committee members) removed — otherwise the "top words" are just the transcript disclaimer.
  • Phrases. Bigrams and trigrams counted over the 111-document markdown mirror, with minimum thresholds.
  • NEWS Coverage side. The same method over 69 news bodies (20–25 Oct) fetched via newsapi.ai and archived at data/news_bodies_20261025.json.
  • Reproducible. Scripts tmp/wfc_ca3.py and tmp/wfc_cov_vs_record.py; raw output in tmp/content_analysis_out.txt. Re-run when the redacted report lands.

5 · The six arguments these numbers support (DATA lines tagged: SITE / NEWS)

A · This is, quantitatively, a maintenance-regime inquiry

DATA: safety in 140 of 243 documents (58%), maintenance in 116 (48%), inspection 732 times across 35 documents.
INFERENCE: the subject is not a single night but a standing regime of upkeep, inspection and sign-off.
CLAIM: the record's own vocabulary frames the disaster as systemic before anyone reads a conclusion.
SUPPORTS: paragraph 2 of the SCMP letter; §2 of the LegCo report; and the expectation that the final report's findings are about regimes, not a villain.

B · The failure chain is physical — and the corpus names it

DATA: foam boards 492 · water tank 350 · scaffolding nets 255 · alarm system 247 · access openings 179 — these out-rank almost every personal name in the archive.
INFERENCE: the evidential weight sits in materials and systems: what was installed, switched off, drained.
CLAIM: "systemic failure" is not a rhetorical flourish — it is the dominant collocation pattern of the record.
SUPPORTS: the letter's numbers; the LegCo feature table; journalists' Case 1 (search foam boards and you are inside the causal story in one step).

C · The four government bodies were visible before the report named them

DATA: buildings department as a fixed phrase 206 times, labour department 170 times; coverage adds icu 64 and department 65 in five days.
INFERENCE: departmental responsibility is a recurring, nameable axis of the testimony — not a late discovery.
CLAIM: when the final report "singles out four government bodies", the corpus already shows which documents to open.
SUPPORTS: forecast predictions 4 and 7 (supervision; adequacy of laws); the LegCo argument that traceability should exist before publication.

D · The tender problem is framed as governance, not crime

DATA: smart tender 167 vs collusion in the low tens; ToR-5 tagging finds 99 documents with tender-irregularity evidence that never use "bid-rigging" or "collusion".
INFERENCE: the record treats the issue as a tool-and-process failure rather than a criminal finding.
CLAIM: the reported line that bid-rigging is "not a major focus" of the final report was predictable from the corpus.
SUPPORTS: forecast prediction 6 (downgraded to "addressed, not central"); journalists' Case 1; paragraph 4 of the 中文 piece.

E · Press and record do not cite each other — the accessibility argument, quantified twice

DATA: coverage's top tokens are government / accountability / reforms / icu / report; the record's are foam boards / water tank / scaffolding nets / maintenance. Overlap ≈ safety, fire.
INFERENCE: two vocabularies describing the same event with no bridge: one asks who is answerable, the other answers what failed.
CLAIM: publication without a shared, machine-readable, question-organised structure means neither side can cite the other — which is why coverage repeats the press release and the archive stays unread.
SUPPORTS: the central argument of the LegCo report (§4) and the ask in both letters: deposit as XML/CSV through data.gov.hk at the point of publication.

F · The thinnest evidence is the residents' remedies

DATA: compensation, rehousing and transitional sit at term frequencies of 1–2 — the lowest of any cluster tested.
INFERENCE: the record is weakest exactly where the public's questions are strongest (cf. 曾詠雪's 21 Oct op-ed on residents' continuing uncertainty).
CLAIM: an absence in a corpus is a finding: the report will not resolve residents' questions, so that story stays with the press and the District Councillors.
SUPPORTS: forecast prediction 10; paragraph 5 of the 中文 piece.

  • Existing powers, already used: the Buildings Department rejected a minor-works licence renewal from the main contractor after disciplinary action (20 April transcript); a registered contractor was "ordered to suspend the registration for three weeks" (8 April); the BD's own website carried the disciplinary-hearing note a witness was asked to check (30 April).
  • The gap is not powers but verification: counsel recorded that "the inspection principles need to be amended… there should be surprise checks" (16 July), and the Committee was asked to "delineate responsibilities… and implement remedial measures" (17 July).
  • Law-enforcement lane, already running: police and ICAC arrests — seven individuals and two companies, manslaughter, money laundering and collusion to defraud (22 June and 17 July transcripts) — 25 charges now before the courts.
  • How to hold them accountable, in one line: the record supports three routes — departmental discipline (existing, evidenced), legislative amendment (ToR 6, 925 hits), and prosecution (running). Our site lets a reader trace each route to the documents: search suspension, disciplinary, surprise checks, delineate.

7 · Caveats

  • Term frequency measures presence, not importance or blame — a phrase can be frequent because it is contested, not because it is proven.
  • Stop-lists were applied by hand; a different list shifts the ranking (raw lists archived with the scripts).
  • The markdown mirror covers 111 documents; word counts come from the full 243-document index.
  • Coverage bodies are aggregator-supplied; paywalled opinion columns (Alice Wu 19 Oct, Regina Ip 25 Oct) were read separately.
  • Re-run on the day the redacted report is published and compare its vocabulary against both tables above.

8 · The 69 news articles — full list and selection rules NEWS

How they were selected (so the analysis can be re-run):

  • Source: newsapi.ai POST /api/v1/article/getArticles — key held privately in issue #8, never in code.
  • Queries (5, declared in advance): Tai Po fire · Wang Fuk Court · 宏福苑 · Tai Po fire report · 宏福苑 報告.
  • Window: 2026-10-22 → 2026-10-25 (the day the report was submitted onward), sorted by date, 50 results per query.
  • Pooling & de-duplication: 131 raw rows pooled → 80 unique by exact title → 69 with usable bodies (article body > 400 characters); 11 dropped for having no/partial body.
  • What counts as an article: any item the aggregator labels as a news or opinion piece — including syndicated copies (e.g. The Star carrying the AP/wire version). Headlines-only rows are excluded from the 69.
  • Reproduce: archive data/news_bodies_20261025.json (committed) · theme classifier codes/news_themes.py · counts tmp/wfc_bodies.py.
  • Limits: aggregator-supplied text (may truncate very long pieces); paywalled opinion columns (Alice Wu 19 Oct, Regina Ip 25 Oct) were read separately and are not in this list; window is deliberately post-submission — earlier coverage is in the Nov 2025 index and issue #8.
